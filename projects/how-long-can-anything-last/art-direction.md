# Art direction: "How Long Can Anything Last?" (video 19, the Lifespan Column)

- **Idea:** the ladder engine of videos 16-18, pointing UP in years on a log scale. One steel rail, one white-cyan marker climbing
  rung by rung (MAYFLY ~1 day to the PROTON's lower limit, > 10^33 yr); a ZEROS counter shows the power of ten of the active rung.
- **Background:** ink #0B0E16 (top) -> #05060A (bottom), faint dust + 2 % grain. No camera; the rail never moves.
- **Rail:** steel, x 250, 10^-4 yr (bottom) to 10^33 yr (y 500); powers every 3 decades in IBM Plex Mono, exponents drawn raised;
  'years · log scale' under it. Above the PROTON the rail BREAKS (zigzag) and a dashed 'forever?' line rises to the top.
- **Marker:** white-cyan #BFF6FF with a soft glow, the ONLY bright accent. No red wall; the Sun / red dwarf tints exist only on the
  two drawn discs in the window inset.
- **Chips:** name Saira Condensed 700 28 px + number Plex Mono 500 26 px; seat with gravity drops on their sounds. The PROTON's
  tick is dashed with an up arrow and its chip says 'lower limit'.
- **Window:** AI plates (tortoise, Greenland shark, bristlecone pine) tagged 'AI impression'; then DRAWN insets tagged 'drawn · not
  to scale': a Sun and a much smaller red dwarf with their lifetimes, then a water detector tank where nothing flashes ('no decay seen').
- **Readout:** '1' + 33 zeros + ' yr' typed glyph by glyph on a card under the window.
- **Honesty:** MAYFLY / TORTOISE / SHARK / PINE / SUN / RED DWARF PROBABLY; YOU and the PROTON's lower limit MEASURED; the closing 'forever' MAYBE.
- **Ending:** a finished line ('Maybe something really does last forever.'), ~1.5 s settle on the resolved chord, CTA 'What's the oldest thing you've ever seen?'.
